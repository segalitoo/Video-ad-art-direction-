The brief

What I set out to do

Take the Bingo Bay workflow from stills to social video, for a portfolio case and for real clients, across every look (3D, live action, UGC, motion graphics) and any generation tool.

What I decided

Video multiplies the cost of every reject. So the look is locked as tokens, every shot starts as a still, and only a kept still is animated. Machines check specs, people judge craft.

What I made

Ten stages with their gates, a lock with four style modes, platform specs, tool adapters, scripts that assemble prompts and check exports, and a Claude skill that runs it all and stops at every gate.

The ten stages

Same stages every time. A person at every gate.

Stages marked new exist because of video. The rest keep the Bingo Bay shape, adapted. The scripts named here are in the repo and tested.

  1. 01Define

    Brief and hook angles

    Claude turns a two-minute intake into a one-page brief and drafts ten hook angles, each with a first frame that reads with the sound off.

    Human gateThe creative lead keeps three hooks and approves one message.

    Tools
    Claude, web search, Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center
    Automated
    Brief draft, hook volume, reference scans
    Stays manual
    Framing the problem, picking the angle

    BottleneckVague briefs. Claude keeps asking until every field is filled.

  2. 02Define

    The lock

    Style frames at volume, narrowed to three directions, then frozen as tokens in brand.dna.yml: mode, world, light, camera, motion, palette, type, sound, hero.

    Human gateThe art director signs the lock. The highest-leverage call in the pipeline.

    Tools
    Claude, Higgsfield, Nano Banana, Midjourney, Figma
    Automated
    Style frames, mode defaults, lock pre-checks
    Stays manual
    The lock itself

    BottleneckDecision fatigue. Three directions, never more.

  3. 03Define

    Script and storyboard new

    The shot list, beat by beat: hook, build, product, proof, payoff, end card. Written as storyboard.yml, so a script can check it.

    Human gateThe creative lead checks the beats. The hook lands inside two seconds.

    Tools
    Claude, Figma, assemble.py --check
    Automated
    Timing sum, super word counts, hook position, platform length limits
    Stays manual
    Pacing, what the viewer feels at each beat

    BottleneckBoards that look finished too early. Words first, pictures at stage 4.

  4. 04Produce

    Keyframes new

    One still per shot, from the assembled prompt. The hero shot goes first; once kept, it becomes the reference attached to every hero shot.

    Human gateThe art director picks one of eight to twelve. Never the first output.

    Tools
    Higgsfield, Nano Banana, Midjourney, Photoshop
    Automated
    Batch generation, cutouts, the log entry
    Stays manual
    Selection, paint-over to the lock

    BottleneckConsistency across shots. One approved hero reference.

  5. 05Produce

    Motion new

    Image-to-video from each kept keyframe. The prompt only says what moves; the frame already carries the look.

    Human gateThe art director picks per shot, against the lock and the shots around it.

    Tools
    Higgsfield, Veo in Google Flow, Kling, Runway, Midjourney video
    Automated
    Clip batches, upscale, the log entry
    Stays manual
    Judging physics, morphing, on-model motion

    BottleneckCost. Keyframes gate motion, so only approved frames get animated.

  6. 06Produce

    Sound and voice

    Music bed, SFX and voiceover from the lock's sound tokens, mixed to about −14 LUFS with a −1 dBTP ceiling, the same ceiling as the Bingo Bay loop.

    Human gateAudio direction clears rights for paid social. Judged on a phone speaker.

    Tools
    Suno (music), Seedance native SFX, Higgsfield voice (ElevenLabs)
    Automated
    Candidates, VO languages, loudness measurement
    Stays manual
    The mix, cut points, rights

    BottleneckLicensing. A pre-cleared kit per brand.

  7. 07Produce

    Copy and on-screen text

    Hooks, supers, CTAs, captions and primary text. Three lines per placement, one ships, and every losing line keeps its reason.

    Human gateThe copy lead picks and trims. Claims never ship unreviewed.

    Tools
    Claude with the voice guide as system prompt
    Automated
    Bulk drafts, word limits from the lock, locales
    Stays manual
    Final voice, anything legal reads

    BottleneckNative review, by market priority.

  8. 08Ship

    Edit new

    Clips, sound and type come together from an edit sheet built from the storyboard. Type and end cards are set by hand, never generated.

    Human gateThe editor or art director owns the cut.

    Tools
    CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, After Effects
    Automated
    Caption drafts, the edit sheet
    Stays manual
    Rhythm, cut points, type

    BottleneckPatching a bad clip in the edit. Send it back to stage 5.

  9. 09Ship

    QA and platform check

    spec_check.py tests ratio, resolution, length, frame rate, loudness, true peak and file size, and saves the hook frame and end frame with the unsafe margins darkened.

    Human gateSign-off for craft, message and brand/legal, including AI disclosure.

    Tools
    spec_check.py, QA checklist
    Automated
    Every spec, the overlay frames
    Stays manual
    Creative judgement on what passed

    BottleneckThe review queue. Only files with no FAIL reach a person.

  10. 10Ship

    Variants and iteration

    matrix.py expands one approved base into hooks × CTAs × platforms, then performance data proposes what to fix.

    Human gateData proposes, a person decides. Someone owns the winner.

    Tools
    matrix.py, crop.py for a free 4:5 cut, platform A/B tests
    Automated
    Variants, reframes, data pulls, logging
    Stays manual
    Deciding what to fix and why

    BottleneckEndless loops. Two cycles, then ship it or kill it.

Loops back to stages 4 to 7. The lock stays frozen unless the art director raises its version.

The lock, for video

A moodboard gets interpreted. A token gets pasted.

The Bingo Bay lock held style, light, form and palette. The video lock adds camera, motion, grade, type, sound and the hero. Every prompt is assembled from it by assemble.py, so nothing drifts quietly between shots or between tools.

Keyframe prompt

STYLE[SHOT]HEROWORLDFORMLIGHTGRADEPALETTETECHFRAME

Only the bracketed slot changes between shots, as the one slot did for the five Bingo Bay characters. FRAME is written from the strictest safe zone across the target platforms.

Motion prompt

[ACTION]CAMERA MOVEMOTIONhold the start frame

Image-to-video models already see the look in the keyframe. Describing it again fights the frame, so the motion prompt says only what moves.

Then, per tool

One master prompt becomes each tool's own form: --no and --ar for Midjourney, a separate negative field for Kling, positive wording only for Runway, sound cues for Veo. Adding a tool is one block in adapters/tools.yml.

Four style modes

One line switches the look. The story needs its own board.

Each mode sets default tokens, negatives and what QA should watch for. A lock picks a mode and overrides only what makes the brand its own.

3d-stylized

3D stylized

Glossy render, rim light, bouncy squash-and-stretch. The Bingo Bay look, in motion.

QA watches: the hero drifting off-model, floating physics.

live-action

Live action

Cinema camera, 50mm, filmic grade, real-world timing.

QA watches: hands, teeth, eyes, labels warping between frames.

ugc

UGC

Phone front camera, window light, talk to camera. The lock controls the brand, not the polish.

QA watches: disclosure of synthetic presenters, lip-sync, spoken claims.

motion-graphics

Motion graphics

Flat shapes and brand type on the beat. Generators make textures only; layouts are built by hand.

QA watches: exact hex values, real type, timing to the music.

Platform rules

Generate once in 9:16. Keep the message in the smallest safe area.

Drawn to scale from platforms/specs.yml. The grey margins are where platform buttons, captions and CTAs sit; the blue box is safe. The 9:16 master uses the strictest margin on each side, so one export is safe everywhere.

Meta's margins are its published unified 9:16 zone (March 2026). TikTok and Shorts publish templates, not pixels, so their margins are conservative third-party values marked verify: true in the specs file. Checked 25 September 2026.

Tools

Any tool. The lock travels with the prompt.

Two engines run inside Claude, split by strength. Figma Weave puts many models behind one connector and keeps the lock as a saved workflow the art director can open. Higgsfield is the cheaper route for the video model, Seedance 2.5, and does what Weave does not. Every paid run is quoted and approved first, and assemble.py prints the budget for a full round before stage 4: about 520 Higgsfield credits (≈ $25) for the 15-second pilot.

Where it jams, and where it compounds

The top four jams

  1. 01The review queue. Pre-checks at stages 3 and 9, and a warning when the matrix passes 24 variants.
  2. 02Consistency in motion. The lock, one hero reference, keyframe first, motion-only prompts.
  3. 03Rights and disclosure. Pre-cleared sound, likeness rules in the brief, AI labels in QA.
  4. 04Endless iteration. Two cycles, then ship it or kill it.

Where it gets faster each time

  • LOGEvery verdict and reason sharpens the next lock version.
